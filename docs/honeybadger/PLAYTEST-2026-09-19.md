# Interactive Playtest: 2026-09-19

## Method

Played two practice matches through the browser's normal buttons, canvas clicks, right-clicks, and keyboard controls. This was exploratory play, not execution of the existing automated browser suite. No direct reducer calls, injected resources, or game-state mutations were used.

Current module published to local `stdbrts-playtest` with `--delete-data=never`; publish reported a successful update without a breaking migration. Local backend on port 3000; Vite at http://127.0.0.1:5173/. Client initially used a short viewport, then 1440x1000, then 990x650. Screenshots were visually inspected during play. No gameplay source changed during this session.

The controller was an AI using browser tools, with pauses for inspecting UI and code. This is evidence of functional interaction and specific UX problems, not a substitute for human feel/balance testing. Match room IDs were not captured; below are displayed simulation times and ticks. No credentials or identity tokens were collected.

## Findings

### 1. Rejected-command explanation is too easy to miss

Second match, about 00:24-00:30, ticks 485-609: clicked cancel production while a soldier was nearly complete. The cancellation activated after completion and correctly rejected with `Production queue is already empty`. The unit remained; no completed-unit refund was observed.

However, the command history displays only `rejected`; the reason is a native hover title. A player can easily interpret this as a broken cancel button, especially on touch. Code confirms the reason is assigned to `row.title` in [main.ts](../../src/main.ts). This is a feedback issue, not an economic correctness bug.

Recommended next small fix: display the reason inline in rejected command rows, with wrapping and a bounded history area. Reproduce the near-completion cancel through the UI and check desktop/narrow layouts. Do not change cancellation timing to hide the problem.

### 2. Short desktop layout leaves too little battlefield

At 990x650, the battlefield measured 240px high with no horizontal overflow. Center-on-HQ gives an inspectable base but clips the nearby mineral line toward the top. The fixed-height command deck consumes much of the window. At 1440x1000 the battlefield measured 576px high and both the base and mining area were much easier to inspect.

This is a usability concern despite passing overflow tests. Investigate a compact command deck or short-height breakpoint, then play rather than checking dimensions alone. Whole-map fit is useful for orientation but makes individual fights very small; it should not be the main combat view.

### 3. Opening pressure needs a deliberate difficulty/playability pass

First match ended in defeat at 01:14, tick 1485. Second defensive opening had lost most mobile units by 01:04 but retained the HQ, turret and outpost; surrendered at about 01:13. The AI visibly built an economy/base and sent attacking units across the map. Combat caused real unit/building losses and the HQ-under-attack alert appeared.

These short sessions do NOT establish that the AI is overpowered: the player paused frequently, trained too little army, and tested controls while under attack. They do establish that practice offers little uninterrupted time to learn construction/production. Consider an explicit relaxed practice option or a sandbox as a separate design choice, rather than silently weakening the default AI. Record first-contact time and available defensive choices in a sustained human match.

## What Worked in Play

- Practice launch, automatic initial mining, rising ore, worker production, and unit-count updates.
- Worker and soldier orders showed a roughly one-second scheduled countdown before activation.
- Barracks placement, worker reassignment, construction-complete alert, producer selection, scout production, and separate barracks rally.
- Mining rally survived reload; a turret build accepted just before reload remained scheduled afterward and completed.
- Army selection, attack-move acceptance, hold acceptance, right-click move, and Ctrl+1 / Escape / 1 selection recall.
- Invalid turret placement returned `Site occupied` and retained placement mode for retry; a clear site succeeded.
- Outpost construction paused after delayed Stop. Right-clicking the unfinished site with the builder issued `construct`; the completed outpost later showed 650 HP.
- A late production cancellation rejected after the unit completed, with the correct reason available in its tooltip.
- First match produced Defeat and a working Return to lobby action. A second practice match started normally.
- Explicit surrender displayed a confirmation dialog and returned to an empty lobby.

These observations do not prove every individual mechanic's timing, resource amount, or combat rule. For example, outpost completion was observed, but improved drop-off efficiency was not measured. Attack-move and hold were issued successfully, but this session did not isolate all acquisition/pursuit rules.

## Session Notes

### Match 1: Production and mixed-army opening

Trained a worker, built barracks near home, assigned HQ ore rally, selected the starting soldier, issued an attack-move toward central terrain, trained another worker/soldier, then selected barracks, trained a scout and assigned ground rally. Used army selection and Hold. Combat losses overtook the planned follow-up attack; the attack button correctly became disabled when no army remained. Defeat at 01:14.

A browser-tool attempt to click that disabled attack button timed out. This was a tool action overtaken by gameplay, not an app hang. An earlier read used a selector matching both hidden lobby and match elements; subsequent reads used the explicit match element. Do not classify either tool error as a game defect.

### Match 2: Defensive construction and reconnect

Started with two additional workers and ore rally, retried a blocked turret placement at a clear site, reloaded during its pending command, verified restored state, trained a soldier, recalled a control group, and moved it toward defense. Attempted a late production cancel, built an outpost, stopped its builder, resumed construction, and trained two more soldiers. Selected the completed outpost to verify completion. Resized to 990x650, inspected the layout, then surrendered through the confirmation dialog.
